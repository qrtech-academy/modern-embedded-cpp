\chapter{Classes}
\label{c2:ch:classes}

{\large\itshape A type that owns its data, and decides how it may be created, copied and
used.\par}

\begin{chapterbox}{In this chapter}
\begin{itemize}
  \item An introduction to object-oriented programming in C++.
  \item Classes and encapsulation.
  \item Constructors and destructors.
  \item File organization for classes: separation into header and source files.
  \item Copy and move semantics.
  \item The keywords \code{explicit}, \code{final}, \code{default}, and \code{delete}.
\end{itemize}
\end{chapterbox}

This chapter is about creating simple classes in C++. You will see what constructors and
destructors are for, what encapsulation means in object-oriented programming, and how a class is
organized across a header file and a source file. You will also meet copy and move semantics, the
keywords \code{explicit}, \code{final}, \code{default}, and \code{delete}, and enumeration classes
(\code{enum class}).

The chapter builds directly on the modern C++ concepts of Chapter~\ref{c1:ch:concepts}, in
particular the structs with member functions of \secref{c1:sec:structs}, and otherwise assumes only
basic C: functions, pointers, and structs.

\Secref{c2:sec:classes1} is an introduction to classes in C++, built around a GPIO driver, and is
the part to read first. \Secref{c2:sec:classes2} is a deeper discussion of classes: the keyword
\code{explicit}, \code{static} members, and how copy and move operations are written. The
exercises in \secref{c2:sec:exercises} are where you write your first class-based drivers, and
their solutions are in the companion repository.
